Virtual Teams: The Absence of Trust and Information Overload

HRMT101 B001: MGMT100 — Human Relations

Virtual Teams have become more and more common, particularly during and after the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic. Virtual Teams can come with many benefits, along with their own set of dysfunctions. This includes the absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, inattention to results, and information overload. This essay will focus on the greatest barrier to a virtual team, the absence of trust, along with ways to minimize information overload.

The Absence of Trust

Trust is the foundation when working in a team. Building and relying on that trust comes with additional challenges when working virtually. Trust does not come without effort, it must be constantly worked on and maintained. Building teams on trust creates a better well-being for team members and improves the teams productivity (Dumitru, 2021). The absence of trust only amplifies the other dysfunctions. If team members do not trust one another, they are more likely to keep ideas to themselves due to a fear of conflict. As the trust issues remain and grow, the lack of commitment will drop, leading to the inattention to results of the project. While these issues continue to pile up, why would a team member take accountability? It would be easier to brush off the inaccurate work or poor outcomes onto other members of the group rather than take accountability.

Virtual teams also can connect team members globally. This can strengthen groups by bringing in talent from around the world, yet cultural differences complicate their development in virtual settings. High-context cultures rely on indirect communication, while low-context cultures prefer direct feedback, creating potential misunderstandings (Horwitz & Santillan, 2012). A high-context team member may hesitate to voice concerns, while a low-context colleague may misinterpret this silence as agreement.

Avoiding Information Overload

Information overload can overwhelm team members and cause more harm than good. Giving too much information at one time can cause this disfunction. It may seem productive to put everything into one email or presentation, but the information will be difficult for team members to digest, and important aspects may get overlooked. It’s important to give people time to understand the key points before moving onto new content (Andersen et al., 2021).

You can avoid information overload further by using clearly defined roles and channels for communicating with one another. Using structured collaboration tools, such as project management software can create clear objectives for each team member. Within project management software there are tools that give you the ability to narrow down the work that has been assigned to you, as well as expanding to see the overall progress of the team. This way, individual contributors and project managers have a clear understanding of what phase a project is in.

Virtual Teams

Virtual Teams are an important aspect of a modern business that come with their own unique challenges. By building trust, adapting to cultural differences, and managing information overload they can be a successful operation to better a company’s mission.

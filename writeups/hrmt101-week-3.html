Human Relations Communication · Week 3

Critical Thinking

Effective communication is an important aspect to successful human relations, influencing collaboration, leadership, and conflict resolution. The Communication Styles Self-Assessment (Case, 1981) offers different descriptions and asks you to spontaneously choose your answer, the results can show you different personality traits you may have that can affect how you work and interact with others. This paper reflects on my assessment results, outlining actionable strategies to enhance my communication style while also leveraging the traits that I have naturally.

What Did I learn

My results were not surprising to me once I added them up. I had dominant categories, Style 4 with 15 points (Idea-oriented) and Style 1 (Action-oriented) with 13 points. This suggests that I prioritize innovation, creativity, and future possibilities (Style 4) while also valuing decisiveness, results, and efficiency (Style 1). My lower scores in Style 3 (People-oriented) with 5 points and Style 2 (Process-oriented) with 3 points highlight potential challenges in prioritizing relational dynamics or systematic processes. I relate to this; I can think of many times where I may get bored at doing the same thing over and over or maybe not want to wait for others to catch up. The positives being I love getting things done, learning and creating new things.

Planned Actions for Improvement

To improve how I communicate, here’s what I’ll focus on:

  • Mixing Creativity with Practicality: I’ll balance big ideas with step-by-step plans to avoid skipping details for excitement.
  • Connecting Better with People: Slow down, listen actively, and prioritize trust-building over rushing tasks.
  • Adapting My Approach: Tailor communication: prep facts for detail-oriented colleagues, start with casual check-ins for relationship-focused ones.

Using This to Communicate Better

Knowing my style helps me play to my strengths while fixing blind spots:

  • Clear, Not Just Creative: Pair bold ideas with clear steps—no more vague plans.
  • Talk Openly, Build Trust: Share openly (even rough ideas) to build trust and team cohesion.
  • Stay Calm, Stay Professional: Pause under pressure—respond calmly, not impulsively.

Conclusion. The Communication Styles Self-Assessment really focused on how I approach tasks, work by myself, and how I work with others. I thrive in fast-paced environments like Information Technology, where quick problem-solving and innovation are essential, but this approach can sometimes lead to conflicts. By listening to others, taking a deep breath, and thinking about projects all the way through, I can improve my style of work and help the teams overall performance.
